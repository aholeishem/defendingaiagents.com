Module 00 · Lesson 09

Twelve questions separate a measurement from a slogan

"Our defence reduces attack success from 62% to 0.8%." That sentence may be a measurement or a slogan, and nothing in it says which. This lesson is the checklist that tells them apart, then a short, honest list of what is not solved.

Twelve questions for any claim

Ask:

  1. What is the threat model? Whose capabilities, whose knowledge, what goal?
  2. Static or adaptive? Were the attacks fixed in advance, or optimised against this defence, with what budget?
  3. What is the denominator? Attacks attempted, attacks that reached the defence, or attacks that succeeded against the undefended system?
  4. What counts as success? The agent started the harmful action, or completed it? A model judge's opinion, or the final state of the environment? (Module 9's four buckets.)
  5. What is the interval? "0 of 50" is not 0%: the one-sided 95% upper bound is 5.8%, and the upper end of the two-sided interval 7.1% (Module 1, lesson 9 teaches both).
  6. What does it cost? Tasks completed with and without the defence; false positives on benign traffic of the shape that will be deployed.
  7. On what population? The benchmark's, or the deployment's? Prose or tool-call payloads?
  8. Against what baseline? An undefended agent, a simple rule, or a naive classifier?
  9. How many runs? A stochastic agent run once is an anecdote. "Succeeds at least once in k tries" (pass@k, 1-(1-p)^k) and "succeeds in all k" (pass$^k$, p^k) are different claims; Module 1, lesson 9 has both.
  10. Who ran it? The defence's author evaluating their own defence has every incentive and every blind spot.
  11. What is out of scope? Read the limitations section first.
  12. Would it survive a motivated attacker with a week? If the honest answer is "nobody has tried", say so.

Nasr et al.'s finding (lesson 7) is a quiet indictment of questions 2 and 12.

What is not solved

An honest map, as of October 2026, with the sources in this course:

Problem State Where
A model that reliably separates instructions from data not available; training narrows the gap (Wallace et al., 2024) and adaptive attacks reopen it (Nasr et al., 2025) lesson 7; Module 4
Detectors that hold up against adaptive attackers the evidence is against it for the families tested (Nasr et al., 2025) Module 9
Structural defences with low utility cost exist (CaMeL, Debenedetti et al., 2025: 77% of tasks vs 84% undefended on AgentDojo); the cost is real and task-dependent lesson 7; Module 11
Composition: security of many agents from security of each no accepted theory in practice; attacks target the routing Module 3, lesson 9
Persistent state (memory, retrieval) as an attack surface a few attacks demonstrated, almost no principled defence Module 5
Evaluating at deployment scale with real base rates each organisation does it alone, with its own data Modules 7, 8

Treat this table as the course's honesty clause. Where a row says the problem is open, a vendor who says it is solved owes you the measurement, with the twelve answers above.

Check yourself

  1. Apply questions 1 to 12 to the CaMeL result as lesson 7 reports it (77% of tasks with provable security, against 84% undefended, on AgentDojo). Which can you answer from lesson 7, and which would send you to the paper?
  2. A vendor claims "zero successful attacks in 10,000 tests". What is the 95% upper bound on the true rate, and what would change your view of the claim?
Answers
  1. From lesson 7: question 6 (77% against 84% of tasks), 7 (AgentDojo, not your deployment), 8 (an undefended system), 10 (the designers evaluating their own design), and 2 in part (a proof holds against adaptive attackers within its threat model). The paper is needed for 1, 3, 4, 5, 9, 11 and 12: the precise threat model, the denominator, what counts as success, the intervals, the number of runs, what "provable" leaves out, and whether anyone else has attacked it.
  2. Zero in 10,000 gives a one-sided 95% upper bound of 1 - 0.05^{1/10{,}000} = 0.030\% (rule of three, 3/n). That bounds the rate only for those 10,000 tests. Fixed or repeated attacks, a vendor marking their own work, or no adaptive attacker would make the number describe the wrong population; independent, adaptive testing with a budget and a stated success criterion would raise my view of it.
